# Findings and Recommendations

## Purpose

The purpose of this stage was to summarize the most important findings identified throughout the analysis and translate those findings into practical recommendations that could support operational improvement and management decision-making.

---

# Overview

The executive dashboard and exploratory analysis provided a detailed view of Riverside Healthcare's financial performance, patient activity, operational efficiency, and treatment outcomes across a six-month reporting period.

The findings presented below are supported by the analysis completed throughout the project and represent the most significant observations identified from the available data.

---

# Key Findings

## Financial Performance

During the six-month reporting period, Riverside Healthcare generated a total revenue of **$317.72K** from **480 hospital visits** involving **117 unique patients**. This resulted in an average revenue of approximately **$2.72K per patient**, providing an overall view of the hospital's financial performance.

Revenue analysis also showed that **Inpatient services generated approximately 64% of total revenue**, while **Outpatient services contributed the remaining 36%**, indicating that inpatient care remained the hospital's primary revenue source.

---

## Diagnosis Performance

Revenue distribution varied across diagnosis categories.

The **Other** diagnosis category generated the highest total revenue during the reporting period. Among the predefined diagnosis categories, **Fracture**, **Hypertension**, **Back Pain**, and **Menstrual Disorder** contributed the highest revenue.

This distribution suggests differences in service demand across diagnosis categories and may assist management when evaluating future resource allocation.

---

## Hospital Performance by Location

Revenue differed across the hospital locations included in the dataset.

**Sheffield** generated the highest revenue during the reporting period, followed by **Liverpool** and **Manchester**, while **Leeds** recorded the lowest revenue.

An additional investigation confirmed that every location reported the same bed capacity. This indicates that differences in revenue were not driven by available bed capacity and were more likely influenced by factors such as patient volume, diagnosis mix, or treatment costs.

---

## Patient Activity

The project recorded **480 hospital visits** from **117 unique patients**, confirming that many patients visited the hospital more than once during the reporting period.

The analysis also showed an average patient waiting time of **29.79 minutes** and an average patient satisfaction score of **3.93 out of 5**, indicating generally positive patient experiences throughout the reporting period.

---

## Insurance Provider Performance

Insurance providers represented an important source of hospital revenue.

The analysis identified **Aviva** as the highest revenue-contributing insurance provider while also covering the largest number of patients.

This finding suggests that patient volume had a greater influence on insurance revenue than the insurance provider itself.

---

## Average Length of Stay

One of the most significant findings emerged while reviewing the Average Length of Stay KPI.

The overall dashboard reported an Average Length of Stay of **1.55 days**. After separating inpatient and outpatient visits, the inpatient Average Length of Stay increased to **6.27 days**.

This demonstrated that combining different visit types within a single KPI can produce misleading results and highlighted the importance of understanding how performance measures are constructed before interpreting them.

---

# Recommendations

## Monitor Revenue Trends Over Time

Extending the reporting period beyond six months would allow management to monitor long-term revenue trends, identify seasonal patterns, and detect operational changes that may influence hospital performance.

---

## Separate Inpatient and Outpatient Performance Metrics

Operational measures such as Average Length of Stay should be reported separately for inpatient and outpatient services.

Doing so provides a more accurate view of hospital resource utilization and supports better operational planning.

---

## Expand Insurance Performance Reporting

Future dashboard versions should include additional insurance metrics such as claim approval rates, reimbursement timelines, outstanding claims, and payment turnaround times.

These measures would provide a broader understanding of revenue collection performance.

---

## Continue Monitoring Patient Waiting Time

Although the analysis found no significant relationship between waiting time and patient satisfaction within this dataset, patient waiting time remains an important operational measure.

Regular monitoring can help identify service bottlenecks before they affect patient experience.

---

## Investigate Revenue Differences Across Locations

Since hospital bed capacity remained constant across every location, future analysis should investigate other variables such as patient volume, referral patterns, diagnosis mix, staffing levels, and service availability to better understand the differences in revenue generation.

---

# Outcome

The project successfully transformed six months of hospital operational data into an interactive reporting solution supported by evidence-based findings and practical recommendations.

Beyond delivering an executive dashboard, the project demonstrated how structured analysis can identify operational patterns, validate assumptions, and support informed business decision-making.
