# Dashboard Design

## Purpose

The purpose of this stage was to design an executive dashboard capable of presenting the most important business information in a clear, organized, and interactive format. The dashboard was designed to allow management to understand hospital performance at a glance while providing the flexibility to explore the data through interactive filtering.

---

# Overview

Before creating any report visuals, I developed a dashboard wireframe to determine the overall layout, identify the KPIs to display, and select the most appropriate visualizations for each business question.

Planning the dashboard before implementation helped ensure that every visual had a clear purpose and contributed to telling the overall business story rather than simply displaying data.

---

# Dashboard Planning

The dashboard layout was planned around three primary objectives:

- Present key business metrics immediately upon opening the report.
- Organize related information into logical sections.
- Minimize unnecessary scrolling and visual clutter.

The wireframe served as the blueprint for the final dashboard and guided the placement of KPI cards, charts, slicers, and supporting visuals throughout the report.

---

# KPI Cards

The first section of the dashboard was dedicated to KPI cards that summarize overall hospital performance.

The KPIs displayed include:

- Total Revenue
- Total Patients
- Total Visits
- Average Revenue Per Patient
- Average Length of Stay
- Average Wait Time
- Average Patient Satisfaction Score

These KPIs provide management with an immediate overview of financial performance, patient activity, and operational efficiency before exploring the detailed visualizations.

---

# Data Visualizations

Each visual was selected based on its ability to communicate a specific business question.

The dashboard includes visualizations that examine:

- Revenue by diagnosis category.
- Revenue by hospital location.
- Revenue by insurance provider.
- Revenue by visit type.
- Treatment outcomes.
- Monthly revenue performance.
- Patient activity.
- Operational performance indicators.

Every chart was chosen to communicate comparisons, trends, or distributions as clearly as possible while avoiding unnecessary visual complexity.

---

# Interactivity

Interactive slicers were added to allow users to filter the dashboard and explore the data from different perspectives.

Depending on the analysis required, users can filter the report by dimensions such as:

- Diagnosis Category
- Visit Type
- Department
- Hospital Location
- Insurance Provider
- Reporting Period

All report visuals respond dynamically to slicer selections because they are connected through the underlying data model.

---

# Design Considerations

Several design principles were followed during dashboard development.

- Maintain a clean and consistent layout.
- Use descriptive visual titles.
- Group related visuals together.
- Display the most important KPIs at the top of the report.
- Minimize unnecessary colors and visual distractions.
- Ensure visual spacing improves readability.
- Design the report for executive-level decision-making.

These decisions improve readability while allowing users to focus on the information that matters most.

---

# Dashboard Validation

After completing the dashboard, each visual was reviewed to verify:

- Correct DAX calculations.
- Accurate visual totals.
- Proper slicer interactions.
- Consistent formatting.
- Clear visual labeling.
- Accurate filter behavior.

This validation ensured that the dashboard communicated reliable information before the exploratory analysis stage began.

---

# Outcome

The completed dashboard transformed six months of hospital operational data into an interactive executive reporting solution capable of supporting financial, operational, and patient performance analysis.

More importantly, planning the dashboard through a wireframe before development resulted in a cleaner layout, improved visual consistency, and a reporting experience that allows users to understand key insights quickly while retaining the ability to explore the underlying data.
