# Project Reflection

## Purpose

The purpose of this reflection is to summarize the key lessons gained throughout the Healthcare Analytics Dashboard project and explain how completing the project influenced my approach to solving business problems with data.

---

# Overview

Developing this project reinforced that successful analytics extends far beyond building dashboards. Every stage, from understanding the business problem to preparing the data, designing the data model, developing KPIs, validating results, and communicating findings, contributed to the overall quality of the final solution.

Rather than viewing these stages as separate tasks, I came to appreciate them as parts of a single analytical workflow where decisions made early in the project directly influenced the reliability of the final results.

---

# Key Lessons

## Understanding the Business Problem Comes First

One of the most important lessons from this project was recognizing the value of understanding the business problem before writing calculations or building report visuals.

Defining the reporting objectives early made it easier to determine which KPIs were needed, how the data should be modeled, and which visualizations would communicate the findings most effectively.

---

## Data Preparation Determines Report Quality

Preparing the data proved to be one of the most important stages of the project.

Cleaning and standardizing the data before building the model helped prevent calculation errors, improved consistency across the reports, and created a repeatable transformation process that can be refreshed as new monthly data becomes available.

This experience reinforced that reliable analysis begins with reliable data.

---

## A Well-Designed Data Model Simplifies Everything

Building the Star Schema demonstrated how a properly organized model simplifies report development.

Separating transactional data from descriptive information made relationships easier to manage, reduced unnecessary complexity, and provided a solid foundation for developing DAX measures and interactive reporting.

---

## Business Questions Drive KPI Development

Creating DAX measures became much easier once each measure was tied to a specific business question.

Rather than developing calculations simply because they were possible, every KPI was created to provide meaningful information that supported financial, operational, or patient performance analysis.

This approach made the dashboard more purposeful and easier to interpret.

---

## Planning Improves Dashboard Design

Creating a wireframe before building the dashboard helped organize the report layout and determine the most appropriate visual for each business question.

Planning the dashboard in advance reduced unnecessary redesign during development and resulted in a cleaner reporting experience focused on communicating insights clearly.

---

## Validate Before Drawing Conclusions

One of the most valuable stages of the project was exploring questions beyond the original dashboard requirements.

Some investigations confirmed expectations, while others demonstrated that the available data did not support the initial assumption. For example, comparing revenue across hospital locations showed that differences could not be explained by bed capacity because every location reported the same capacity.

This reinforced the importance of validating findings with evidence before presenting conclusions or recommendations.

---

# Looking Ahead

Completing this project has strengthened my approach to future analytics work.

Rather than beginning with visualizations, I now approach projects by first understanding the business objective, preparing the data carefully, organizing the model, developing business-focused KPIs, and validating results before communicating recommendations.

This structured workflow not only improves the quality of the analysis but also makes the final reporting more meaningful for decision-makers.

---

# Conclusion

The Healthcare Analytics Dashboard represents more than the completion of a Power BI project. It demonstrates a structured approach to solving business problems through data.

More importantly, the project reinforced that effective analytics is built on careful preparation, thoughtful modeling, evidence-based analysis, and clear communication. These principles will continue to guide how I approach future analytics projects regardless of the industry or dataset.
